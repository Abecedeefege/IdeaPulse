Tech Support House Calls for Seniors

A patient, background-checked technician who comes monthly to fix the printer, de-scam the inbox, and set up video calls with the grandkids — paid for by the adult kids.

difficultyUnder $500 to startFirst dollar: 1–2 weeks
#seniors#services#membership#trust

The fastest-growing demographic owns the most devices they can't operate, and their adult children — the actual buyers — live in another city worrying about scam calls. A membership-based house-call service (monthly visit plus unlimited phone help) monetizes patience and trust, two things Geek Squad structurally cannot deliver.

Market

Tens of millions of seniors live independently with smartphones, smart TVs, patient portals, and password chaos, while elder-targeting fraud causes billions in annual losses — making 'scam-proofing' a concrete, urgent sell to adult children. Competitors are big-box services with rotating staff; the relationship-based local model has near-zero organized competition in most cities.

Monetization

Memberships at $79–149/month (one visit plus remote support) sold primarily to adult children as 'peace of mind' gifts; one-off visits at $95–125 as the funnel. Thirty members is $30–50K/year per technician with high retention — clients keep the service for years and refer their friends at bridge club.

First steps

  1. 01Get background-checked and create a one-page 'what we help with' menu in large, friendly type.
  2. 02Pitch two senior-living communities and one church group on a free monthly help desk to build trust and a waitlist.
  3. 03Market to the adult children on local Facebook groups and Nextdoor: 'I'm the tech person for your mom.'
  4. 04Build the scam-proofing checklist (call screening, email filters, financial alerts) as your signature service.

Risks

  • Trust is the entire business; one bad hire or incident is catastrophic — grow slower than demand suggests.
  • Scaling beyond yourself requires finding patient, vetted technicians, which is genuinely hard.
  • Some demand is rescue-driven, not recurring; the membership pitch must emphasize prevention.

Who this is for

A patient communicator who finds the work genuinely meaningful — empathy is the product, tech skills are table stakes.
